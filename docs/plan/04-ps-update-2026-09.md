# The problem statement changed: what it means, measured

PS 26067 was updated. The theme is now **Disaster Management**, and four dataset links were
added that were not there before. Everything below was measured from this machine on
**2026-09-01**. Nothing is recalled and nothing is assumed.

This file follows the same rule as `03-requirement-gaps.md`: **a source that cannot share the
demo's timeline is not a source**, and half of what follows is a decision *not* to build
something, with the measurement that settles it.

### 2. `incois_argo_10day_McCreary` - the find

This was sitting on INCOIS's own ERDDAP the whole time and nobody had looked at it. There are
17 datasets on that server; this is one of them.

```
https://erddap.incois.gov.in/erddap/griddap/incois_argo_10day_McCreary
```

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Method | Kessler-McCreary, an **independent analysis** of the same Argo floats |
| Grid | 24 levels x 60 lat x 90 lon, 1 degree. **Identical to the VAM grid we already read** |
| Time | 921 steps, **2001-01-10 to 2026-07-30** |
| VAM, for comparison | 813 steps, 2004-01-10 to **2026-07-30** |

Twelve variables, measured off the `.dds`:

```
T_ANALYZED  S_ANALYZED   the analysis
T_MEAN      S_MEAN       the statistical mean
T_STDEV     S_STDEV      INCOIS's own standard deviation
T_RMSE      S_RMSE       INCOIS's own RMSE against the analysed mean
T_ROIOBS    S_ROIOBS     observation count in the region of influence
T_BOXOBS    S_BOXOBS     observation count in the 1x1 box
```

Three things follow, and all three are close to free because it is the same host, the same
protocol and the same adapter:

1. **INCOIS's own observation count, on the live timeline.** This platform computes its own
   Observation Coverage from the Argo casts. Now it can be shown beside the provider's own
   count, which validates our method against theirs instead of asking anyone to take it on
   trust.
2. **INCOIS's own uncertainty.** `STDEV` and `RMSE` are a published error estimate. Today the
   honest claim is "we show where there is no evidence". It becomes "and here is the provider's
   own error, beside ours."
3. **A second independent analysis of the same water.** Where two analyses of the same floats
   disagree is a real uncertainty signal, and it needs no new source, no account and no new
   science.

## The derived fields, prototyped and checked

The new theme wants quantities INCOIS used to publish and stopped. All of them come out of the
temperature and salinity this project already has. Prototyped against the native grid for
**2026-07-30**, the last timestep, in peak south-west monsoon:

| Location | D26 (m) | TCHP (kJ/cm2) | MLD (m) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Arabian Sea, Oman upwelling (17.5 N, 58.5 E) | **9.7** | **1.1** | 20.6 |
| Arabian Sea, central (15.5 N, 65.5 E) | 72.7 | 58.3 | 54.0 |
| Bay of Bengal, north (17.5 N, 88.5 E) | 64.3 | 53.3 | **25.5** |
| Bay of Bengal, central (13.5 N, 87.5 E) | 77.2 | 76.8 | 52.2 |
| Equatorial Indian (0.5 N, 75.5 E) | 71.3 | 89.8 | 15.6 |
| Lakshadweep (10.5 N, 72.5 E) | 82.2 | 64.8 | 12.2 |

Definitions used: **D26** is the depth where temperature crosses 26 degC, linearly interpolated
between levels. **TCHP** is Leipper and Volgenau's heat potential, `rho * cp * integral(T - 26)`
from the surface to D26, with rho 1026 and cp 4178. **MLD** is de Boyer Montegut's density
threshold, sigma-theta 0.03 kg/m3 above the 10 m value, computed from the TEOS-10 density this
project already carries.

**Why these numbers are believable.** The Oman row is the check that matters. In peak monsoon,
wind drags cold water to the surface off Oman, so the 26 degC isotherm should be almost at the
surface and there should be no cyclone fuel at all. It reads **9.7 m and 1.1 kJ/cm2**. Separately,
and by a completely different method, this platform's own anomaly detector already flags that
exact water at that exact step as the strongest cold feature in the block: **-6.65 degC peak,
12.5-20.5 N, 55.5-60.5 E, 381,106 km2**. Two independent methods agreeing on one physical event
is the strongest evidence available without ground truth.

The Bay of Bengal rows carry the other expected signature: a **shallow mixed layer over a deeper
warm layer** in the north (25.5 m MLD under a 64.3 m D26), which is the fresh-water barrier layer
the Bay is known for.

**The limitation to state rather than hide.** The levels are 5, 10, 20, 30, 50, 75, 100 m and so
on, so anything derived from a threshold crossing inherits that spacing. D26 near 70 m is
interpolated across a 25 m gap. MLD is more sensitive still, because the criterion bites in the
10 to 30 m range where there are only three levels. The Lakshadweep MLD of 12.2 m is the row that
most deserves a second look against the literature before it ships. INCOIS computed their own
value-added products from this same grid, so the practice is theirs as much as ours, but that is
an explanation and not an excuse.

## Priority

Ordered by value per hour of work. Effort figures are estimates, not measurements.

### P0 - build these first

**1. The disaster-management derived Fields: D26, TCHP, MLD, ILD, barrier layer thickness.**
*Estimate 6 to 10 hours.*
The theme is now Disaster Management and the platform currently has no field that a hazard
forecaster would name. These five are the ones they would. They need **no new source, no
account and no new dependency** beyond the `gsw` already in the tree, they are prototyped above
and come out physical, and INCOIS's own value-added series proves these are the quantities they
want, while its 2019 end date proves nobody is serving them. Each becomes a `FieldSpec` with its
own palette, its own guide entry and its own isosurface meaning, exactly as ADR 0010 requires.
TDD applies: these are derived Fields.

**2. Read `incois_argo_10day_McCreary`.**
*Estimate 4 to 6 hours.*
Same host, same protocol, same adapter, live to 2026-07-30. It brings INCOIS's own observation
counts, their own RMSE and standard deviation, and a second independent analysis of the same
water. It turns "trust our coverage field" into "here is ours beside the provider's own", and it
is the cheapest credibility in the entire backlog.

### P1

**3. A `GliderSource` adapter, run against the FTP the PS names, publishing the honest result.**
*Estimate 5 to 8 hours.*
The PS names gliders in the gaps list, in the core requirements and in the dataset links. Three
times. "Not built" is the wrong answer to give three times. Build the adapter against
`ftp.ifremer.fr/ifremer/glider/v2/`, prove the seam carries it, and then say what the data says:
one glider, two deployments, 2,876 profiles, newest cast 2022-10-14, nothing since. Either ship
the 2022 deployment behind an explicit historical label or ship the finding. **The gap is India's
glider programme, not our adapter**, and that is a far stronger sentence than a promise.

**4. Retheme the deck and the product framing to Disaster Management.**
*Estimate 2 to 3 hours, and partly done already.*
Slide 5 already leads with cyclone and hazard forecasters. Once item 1 lands, that section stops
being an argument about what the tool could support and starts naming fields it actually has.

### P2

**5. Current vectors as real numbers, through a free Copernicus account.**
*Estimate 6 to 8 hours.*
This is the last core requirement still only partly met. `GLOBAL_MULTIYEAR_PHY_001_030` carries
`uo`, `vo` and `mlotst` at 1/12 degree, overlapping nine of the twelve timesteps. **The cost is a
credential in the bake**, which weakens the "no account anywhere" line to "no account at demo
time". That is a real trade and it is the user's call, not ours. If it is taken, the currents
stop being a picture and become a Field with arrows, values under the cursor and a place in the
variable selector, and ADR 0011 gets superseded rather than quietly broken.

**6. A working log scale on the colourbar.**
*Estimate 3 to 4 hours.*
The PS names "log/linear scale" explicitly. It was cut because the log warped the water while the
colourbar stayed linear, and the two disagreed. That was a bug, not a reason. Applying the
transfer to both the shader and the colourbar together fixes it, and ADR 0010 gets amended
rather than left looking like a refusal.

### P3

**7. Write the new measurements into the README compliance table and the provenance page.**
*Estimate 2 hours.* Chiefly: the glider row now cites the PS's own index and its own numbers,
and the LAS finding replaces the guess about what INCOIS publishes.

**8. Say in one line that we read the Argo archive the PS names.** *Estimate 15 minutes.* We do,
through Ifremer's ERDDAP rather than their FTP, and for a stated reason.

## Still not building, and why

| Clause | Decision | The measurement behind it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **OGC WCS** | No | No maintained pure-Python WCS server. OPeNDAP is what this community uses and we serve it. Unchanged from `03-requirement-gaps.md`. |
| **Machine-learning derived products** | No | Twelve timesteps is not a training set, and a neural gap-filler would paint over the 6.0% of the block that is the most honest thing in the tool. Unchanged. |
| **CTD sections** | No | Newest GO-SHIP section in this box is April 2025. Lower return than everything above. Unchanged. |
| **HF-radar and ADCP** | No | India's are behind a login at `services.incois.gov.in`, which does not resolve from this machine. A data-policy fact, not an architecture gap. Unchanged. |
| **INCOIS LAS as a data source** | No | Catalogue reads fine; the OPeNDAP endpoint returned zero bytes at 90 s and again at 240 s. Read the catalogue, do not depend on the server. **New.** |
| **Godas** | No | `Godas 2025` ends 2025-05-22, eleven months before this bake's window. **New.** |
| **Copernicus WMTS for the reanalysis** | Not needed | We already carry current arrows as a Copernicus image. Item 5 supersedes this if it is taken. |
