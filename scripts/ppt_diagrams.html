The five gaps PS 26067 names

Their words, then what is built and running

Gap 1
"No web-based, platform-independent 3D rendering with depth-resolved volumetric views."
A GPU ray-marched water column, 5 m to 2000 m, in any WebGL2 browser. Public and live today.
Gap 2
"No unified display of instrument profile data alongside model fields."
237 instruments drawn inside the water. Click one and the model is scored against it: 100,905 depth points compared.
Gap 3
"Absence of interactive controls for variable selection, depth-slice navigation, time-step animation, customizable colorbars."
All four, live. 15 variables in five groups, two depth handles, 12 analyses animated, both colourbar handles and a log or linear scale.
Gap 4
"Inability to ingest new data streams or model variables without significant re-engineering."
One adapter class per provider. 8 sources read through the same seam, plus a ninth that reads a NetCDF file a visitor drops on the page.
Gap 5
"Lack of tools to support intuitive, rapid understanding of complex 3D ocean phenomena."
Every control explains itself in plain language. A 21-step guided tour over all 43 controls, eight plain-word questions, a one-click cyclone setup, and 121 unusual bodies of water found for you.

Innovation and uniqueness

Four things this does that a stack of flat maps cannot

01 · Quantified
The disagreement is a number
Not "the model looks off". The float in the screenshot beside this agrees to 0.17 °C over 996 depths, RMS 0.47 °C. Turn the bias map on and all 230 instruments are ranked by that same figure - and the worst is a moored buoy the analysis never ingested, at 1.66 °C. Agreement and disagreement are the same measurement.
02 · Honest
It shows where there is no evidence
Observation Coverage is a variable in its own right: how many Argo casts stand behind each point. 9.9% of the block has none, and the picture says exactly where.
03 · Automatic
It finds the odd water for you
121 unusual bodies across 12 analyses, each with a z-score, a depth band, a footprint in km² and the cast count behind it. Isolate one and the rest of the block clips away.
04 · DisciplinedThe picture and the truth are kept apart
The GPU gets a quantised byte texture. Every number a person reads, and every byte served over OPeNDAP, WMS or NetCDF, comes from the native grid instead.
And it refuses to invent. Geostrophic current speed was built, measured at 0.16 m/s for the Somali Current in peak monsoon against a real 1.5 to 2.5 m/s, and deleted. There is no machine-learning gap-filler painting over the 9.9% nobody measured, because a smooth believable temperature there would hide the one honest hole in the picture.

Zone 4  Delivery
Browser  ←  Volume and GridThree.js · WebGL2 · GLSL ES 3.00One scene for the globe and the ray-marched block. The picture comes from the Volume; every number on screen comes from the Grid shipped beside it.
Static bake  ←  both71.1 MB, committed15 Fields over 12 analyses. The demo makes zero network calls, checked in CI on every push.
REST API  ←  GridFastAPI · 14 routesLive collocation for any instrument, any column of water, any section line, and the one route that accepts anything: a NetCDF file.
Open standards  ←  GridDAP2 · CF-1.8 · WMS 1.3.0OPeNDAP with constraint expressions, self-describing NetCDF, and WMS for the fields that exist nowhere else.
The rule that governs all of it. Every number a human or a machine reads comes from the Grid. The Volume is only ever a picture. This matters most at the API, because a consumer pulling NetCDF over the wire cannot see that they have been handed a quantised, depth-warped, land-filled approximation. Two modules break the one-copy rule and both are held to it: the drift integrator and the vertical section run in the browser as well as in Python, because the demo runs with the API off - and a probe runs the shipped browser module against the pipeline's and fails on disagreement. Measured: drift median 0.331 km over 101 days, section worst gap 5.07e-5 degC over 1,102 values.

Methodology

From eight public endpoints to a block of water you can fly into

01
Fetch
Subset each source at the server, over one region and one window. Every endpoint tested and dated before it was trusted.
02
Check
Quality flags read per channel, so a float with a dead salinity sensor still gives its good temperature. Land masked, never back-filled into the sea.
03
Derive
Density from TEOS-10, the five cyclone-hazard fields INCOIS stopped publishing in 2019, a temperature anomaly, two observation counts and 121 unusual bodies of water located automatically.
04
Bake
Native grids saved for the API and the standards; byte volumes packed for the GPU; depth sheets and column drapes left unquantised. 71.1 MB, committed, so the demo needs no network.
05
Render and compare
Ray-march the block in the browser, draw every instrument where it truly was, score the model against each cast, and publish the score: typical temperature gap 0.19 degC, and 0.75 degC against the nine buoys INCOIS did not assimilate.

Challenges met, and how

Each of these was hit during the build. Each was measured, then resolved.

1
Risk · the data disappears
Source endpoints die or refuse you. HYCOM and NOAA CoastWatch both timed out from our network on day one. INCOIS's own chlorophyll ends in 2006 and 2020, their currents and their cyclone-hazard products in 2019, and the last glider left this basin on 14 Oct 2022. Each is measured, dated and either replaced or reported.
→
Resolved
Every endpoint is tested, dated and written down, and the adapter seam makes a swap one class. We moved to INCOIS's own public ERDDAP, which made the demo end-to-end INCOIS instead of a substitute.
2
Risk · the instruments lie
Real sensors fail in the water. One float in this region reported about 20 PSU, fresher than the Baltic Sea and impossible here, and the global quality standard let it through.
→
Resolved
Two layers of quality control. Argo's own flags are read per channel, so a float with a dead salinity sensor still gives its good temperature, then a regional floor catches what the global rule passes. Reading the flags properly took usable floats from 93 to 228.
3
Risk · the hardware cannot cope
A forecasting desk is not a gaming PC. Ray-marching every pixel of a 3D texture is heavy, and the machines this has to run on have no discrete graphics card.
→
Resolved
4 bytes per voxel, a ray-step slider, and textures freed as the animation plays. The whole thing was built and tested on integrated graphics with 2 GB of shared memory.
4
Risk · missing data can lie
The gaps invent a phenomenon. A GPU blends neighbouring cells before the shader sees them, so blending "no data" into the sea paints a fake cold strip along every coast, and it looks exactly like real upwelling that a forecaster would act on.
→
Resolved
A separate channel records where the ocean actually is. Masked cells are back-filled from their nearest real neighbour so they are harmless to blend against, and land can never contribute a value at any zoom.
5
Risk · a plausible wrong number
Worse than no number at all. A derived current field looked finite, physical and entirely convincing on screen, and a judge or a forecaster would have had no way to tell it was wrong.
→
Resolved
Measured against reality, then deleted. It gave 0.16 m/s for the Somali Current in peak monsoon against a real 1.5 to 2.5. Currents now come from Copernicus's own analysis, held to the same test and passing it: 2.94 m/s at 9.5°N, 51.5°E on 30 July, which is the Somali Current core in the month it peaks.
Every risk above was met during the build and resolved, and each resolution is recorded with the measurement that settled it. This is a record, not a forecast.

Who it helps

Every one of these is an existing INCOIS operational mandate

INCOIS · Ocean Valley, Hyderabad Ministry of Earth Sciences. One tab instead of three programs.
01
Cyclone and hazard forecasters
A storm's fuel is the heat below the surface, not on it. Cyclone heat potential is measured down to the depth of an isotherm, and this draws that isotherm as a solid surface you can fly around and watch dome over four months.
02
Search and rescue
See the real water structure inside the actual search box in seconds, with the nearest instruments and what they measured, instead of opening three separate programs.
03
Fisheries advisories
INCOIS already sends potential fishing zone advisories to lakhs of fishermen. Those depend on fronts and on mixed-layer depth, and both become objects you can point at rather than things inferred from stacked flat maps.
04
Climate and ocean-state monitoring
A temperature anomaly field with unusual bodies of water found automatically, and beside it an honest map of where no observation stands behind the analysis at all.
05
Students, the public, and policy
A browser, no install, no login, no cost, a guided walk through every control, and an exhibition mode that resets itself. The same tool that a forecaster uses is the one a school class flies into the Bay of Bengal with.

Benefits of the solution

Social · economic · environmental

Social
Opens in any browser, with no install, no login and no cost, so the same tool serves a forecaster's console and a school classroom. Every control explains in plain language what it does and whether it changed the science or only the picture, so the tool teaches while it is used. Directly supports the outreach, exhibition and e-learning mandate the problem statement asks for.
Economic
No licence, no GPU cluster, and no new data collection. No account to view or use it; one free Copernicus account to rebuild the data. 71.1 MB of static files plus one small Python service is the entire deployment. It makes the data INCOIS already produces, and already pays for, do more work, and it cuts a three-program comparison down to one browser tab. Adding a new sensor costs one class, not a rebuilt tool.
Environmental
Chlorophyll from 52 biogeochemical floats sits beside the physics. 121 unusual bodies of water are found automatically across the four months, each with a z-score, a depth band and a footprint in km², which is how anomalous warm water gets noticed early. And 9.9% of the block is marked as having no evidence behind it rather than quietly filled in with a guess.
What changes for the person doing the work: "the model looks off" becomes 0.72 °C warm across 522 depths, RMS 1.72 °C - Argo 1902681, its 11 July 2026 cast against the 10 July analysis.
